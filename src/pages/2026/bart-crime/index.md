The numerator combines homicide, rape, robbery, aggravated assault, and electronic theft. Electronic robbery is already included in robbery and is counted once. Rates are calculated as the combined count divided by passenger trips, multiplied by one million. A fiscal year starts in July: FY26 Q4 is April–June 2026.

**This is a reconstruction, not an exact transcription of the news graphic.** BART's [September 2026 article](https://www.bart.gov/news/articles/2026/news20260908) does not publish its underlying table. Retrospective rates, original quarterly rates, and category counts do not always reconcile. For example, the currently downloadable FY26 Q4 report lists 9 robberies, 40 aggravated assaults, no rapes or homicides, and 10 electronic thefts: 59 combined crimes. With 16,030,982 trips, that gives 3.68 per million. Its security page rounds this to 3.7. The article's graphic differs from this reconstruction. This page preserves the category counts in the cited reports rather than adjusting them to match the image.

Sources: [BART quarterly performance reports](https://www.bart.gov/about/reports) and [monthly police reports](https://www.bart.gov/about/police/reports) for crimes, and [monthly ridership workbooks](https://www.bart.gov/about/reports/ridership) for trips. The JSON identifies each report, section, workbook, sheet, monthly total, and extraction method. Passenger totals sum the three monthly grand totals on the **Total Trips** sheets, including sheets named **Total Trips OD**. They measure trips, not unique passengers.

The pre-pandemic reconstruction uses monthly police reports for FY19 and retrospective quarterly category tables for FY20. The FY20 counts agree with page 40 of the [FY21 Q2 presentation](https://www.bart.gov/sites/default/files/docs/Quarterly%20Service%20Performance%20Review%20%E2%80%93%20Second%20Quarter%20Fiscal%20Year%202021%20-%20Presentation.pdf); the JSON cites later tables with the same values. Monthly electronic-theft counts are the printed **Theft By Snatching** values, not the **Force or Fear** values already covered by robbery.

FY19's other four categories use retrospective year-to-date tables, subtracting adjacent quarter-end snapshots where necessary. **Exact arithmetic does not make these incident-level quarterly totals:** revisions to earlier months can affect a difference between snapshots. For example, a revised 2018 robbery total changes reconstructed FY19 Q2 robberies from 114 to 110; revised January–March 2019 figures change FY19 Q3 homicides from 0 to 1 and rapes from 1 to 5. The JSON retains each contributing source, calculation, and caveat. The monthly reports warn that their database counts are unaudited and subject to revisions.

**July 2019 marks a source change, not a known offense-definition change.** The dashed marker and gap separate monthly police counts from retrospective quarterly counts. Electronic-theft totals match in the overlapping FY20 Q1–Q2 reports, but other categories differ: monthly FY20 Q1 aggravated-assault counts give 25 rather than the quarterly table's 23; FY20 Q2 gives 107 robberies and 27 aggravated assaults rather than 109 and 26. Treat comparisons across this source boundary cautiously.

The published rate's definition also changed: the FY21 Q1 report lists four offenses, while FY21 Q2 adds electronic theft. That does **not** introduce another category break here, because the reconstruction includes electronic theft in every quarter, including the older ones. This harmonizes the category list, not every aspect of reporting or later revisions.

FY21–FY22 crime counts come from the FY22 Q2 and Q4 category tables. FY23 and FY24 Q1 counts come from the embedded chart data in the FY24 Q1 presentation (served with a `.pdf` filename, but actually a PowerPoint file). FY24 Q2–Q3 and FY24 Q4–FY25 Q4 counts were read from the bars in the FY24 Q4 and FY25 Q4 reports and rounded to the nearest whole crime; treat those seven quarters as digitized estimates. FY26 counts are printed in each quarter's security section. These source vintages can include later revisions to earlier quarters.

**Reporting changed in January 2025.** BART explains that “NIBRS records each offense that occurs during a given incident,” while UCR records one. A dashed vertical line marks the change, and the crime lines break rather than connect across it because the two systems are not directly comparable. See [BART's reporting explanation](https://www.bart.gov/about/police/reports). Changes in these rates alone do not establish the effect of fare gates or policing.
